# voice-editor

Claude doesn't sound robotic because it is bad at writing. It sounds robotic because it doesn't
know how YOU talk, and it falls back on the same safe phrases everyone else gets. This skill
fixes both.

## Step 1: Build voice.md (once)

If `voice.md` doesn't exist in the project, ask for three to five things the person wrote or said
themselves: captions they're proud of, a transcript of a video, emails to customers. Not AI drafts.

Read them and write `voice.md` with these sections:

- **Who they talk to.** One sentence.
- **How they sound.** Five short traits, each with a real line from their samples as the example.
- **Sentence habits.** Typical length, contractions or not, how they start sentences, how they end posts.
- **Words they use.** Ten or so words and phrases that are clearly theirs.
- **Never say.** Their personal banned words, plus the shared list below.
- **What they won't claim.** Results, numbers, or promises they never make.

Show the file and ask them to fix anything that's wrong. Their edits beat your guesses.

## Step 2: The never-say list

Every edit removes these unless the person really talks this way (check their samples).

**Words:** delve, unlock, unleash, elevate, seamless, leverage (as a verb), game-changer,
revolutionary, robust, tapestry, testament, pivotal, realm, meticulous, supercharge, harness.

**Openers:** "Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "In today's fast-paced world", "Have you ever
wondered", "Hey guys, welcome back", "In this video".

**Shapes:**
- "It's not X, it's Y" and "not just X, but Y". Say Y.
- One-line closers that repeat the point: "Let that sink in.", "Read that again.", "That's the real win."
- Fragments in a row: "Simple. Fast. Effective."
- Questions you answer yourself: "Why does this matter? Because..."
- Everything in threes when there are really two things, or four.
- Em dashes everywhere. Use a period or a comma.

Let the person add to this list. Put their additions in `voice.md`.

## Step 3: Edit a draft

1. **Freeze the facts.** Before changing anything, list every number, price, date, name, quote,
   and link in the draft. These must come out the other side unchanged.
2. **Cut the robot.** Remove every never-say hit. Rewrite the sentence around its point; don't swap
   one stock phrase for another.
3. **Put the voice in.** Match their sentence length, their openers, their words. Read it as them.
   If a line is something they would never say out loud, rewrite it.
4. **Check the facts.** Compare against the frozen list. Anything changed, dropped, or added gets
   fixed or flagged as a question. Never invent a result to make the copy stronger.
5. **Read it aloud.** Any line you trip over, split or rewrite.

## Step 4: Score it

Score 1-5 on each, total out of 25. Under 20, fix the lowest line and score again.

1. **Sounds like them.** Someone who knows them would believe they wrote it.
2. **Clean.** Zero never-say hits.
3. **Specific.** Real things, real numbers from the source, nothing vague.
4. **Facts intact.** Every frozen fact matches.
5. **One clear ask** at the end, if the piece has one.

## What to hand back

The edited copy first. Then a short report: the score line, the facts kept (for example 6/6),
the never-say hits removed, and any questions about facts.

End with: **post this, or change it?**
